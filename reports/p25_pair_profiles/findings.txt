Pair-quality comparison for UAL/AAL and UPST/COIN

Read-only analysis of all 345 cached roster pairs. No trades, models, costs,
or screens changed. History: 2018 through August 29, 2025, where available.
UPST/COIN has a shorter overlapping stock history: 46 known monthly screens,
versus UAL/AAL's 85. Historical medians describe each pair's available data;
the roster median is equal weight across pair medians, not across all days.

Beta is A-on-B based on 126 preceding return observations, shifted one day
and frozen at monthly formation. It is neither beta to the market nor a new
daily estimate. Correlation uses the same return sample. IV is the mean
selected approximately 30D 25-delta put BS IV across both names. RV is the
mean annualized 20-session close-to-close RV used by the existing state.

Directional beta can conceal a volatility mismatch
For the same return sample, beta(A|B)=corr(A,B)*sigma(A)/sigma(B).
Consequently:
UAL/AAL formation-sample RV ratio =1.078; reverse beta(AAL|UAL)=0.738.
UPST/COIN formation-sample RV ratio=1.686; reverse beta(COIN|UPST)=0.303.
The ratios are derived from the formation sample, NOT from the20-session
RV state. The current gate checks only the stored A-on-B beta, so it does
not require near-one beta in both directions or equal return volatility.

IV co-movement: sparse-data diagnostic
With126-session rolling correlations and at least84 paired observations,
historical median IV-level correlation is0.942 UAL/AAL and0.516 UPST/COIN;
IV-change correlation is0.690 and0.040, respectively. Only64 roster pairs
have any valid IV-level rolling estimate and40 have any valid IV-change
estimate. UAL/AAL has434/147 valid rolling observations, UPST/COIN292/51;
these windows overlap and are not independent observations.
At the specific losing-entry dates neither pair has enough paired quotes
to meet that84-observation threshold:70/81 level observations and48/64
change observations, respectively. Missing quotes are not filled. Therefore
these IV-correlation results are coverage-limited and should not be presented
as precise full-universe rankings or signal-date observations.

Interpretation
UPST/COIN gives a clearer pair-quality warning than UAL/AAL. UAL/AAL's
strong co-movement did not protect against the later AAL-specific price jump;
its recent RV was elevated at entry. Beta near one alone is insufficient to
identify similar volatility exposures. A possible next test is a symmetric
pair-quality/risk input plus concentration controls, using causal rolling
evaluation rather than excluding these names because their losses are known.
