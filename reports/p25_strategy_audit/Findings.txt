P25 STRATEGY INVESTIGATION — THREE QUESTIONS
Completed on October 7, 2026

The current tree is a defensible research benchmark. Its superiority to simple
constant sizing is not established with confidence. The stronger concerns are
the inherited opportunity universe and the concentration of profits in a few
pairs and periods. This investigation supports improving those foundations
before treating additional model complexity as the main solution.

We verified the current validation-selected 27-input policy, its 90 monthly
models, all 608 qualified proposals and 207 executed pair trades. Every one of
273 files in the reference archive passed its recorded hash check. Its funded
NAV, trades and decisions match the previously reproduced baseline. The older
33-input submission policy is a different model and is not the baseline here.

All comparisons below use $10 million funded accounts. The earlier period is
2018–2021 and the later period is January 2022–August 29, 2025. Monthly fits use
only completed past outcomes, but this historical period has already been
examined repeatedly. These are exploratory historical comparisons, not a new
untouched holdout. Portfolio inventory continues across monthly and yearly
boundaries. The current strategy sizes new option entries, holds their contract
quantities until expiry, and adjusts the stock delta hedge daily.

1. DOES THE TREE PROVIDE A JUSTIFIED POSITION-SIZING RULE?

A single tree is a coherent way to map entry information to a piecewise sizing
policy. The leaf labels are local partitions of that month's model; they are
not eight permanent economic regimes. Using one tree is not itself a defect.

The key question is whether those partitions add enough value to justify the
estimation and optimization. We compared the current tree/CEM pipeline with
three existing constant-size controls and two new, predeclared alternatives:

Sizing pipeline                Earlier Sharpe   Later Sharpe
Current tree + CEM                   0.573           1.263
Constant 50%                        0.437           1.007
Constant 75%                        0.449           0.944
Constant 100%                       0.478           1.055
Historical spread-rank rule          0.464           0.815
Regularized linear score            0.421           0.890

The rank rule uses completed-training spread-strength terciles. The linear
model combines the same 27 inputs using ridge regression, then converts its
score to the same 50/75/100% action ladder using training-only terciles. We
fitted 90 monthly linear models and ran two new funded portfolio replays.
Both alternatives preserve the opportunity stream, costs and risk rules.
Actual admissions can differ because sizing changes inventory and headroom.

The tree has the highest Sharpe in both periods among these six pipelines.
Later profit above cash is $248,673 for the tree, $164,770 for the rank rule,
and $179,745 for the linear rule. The new alternatives perform worse in all
three tested time-block uncertainty calculations.

However, the constant 100% rule is the strongest constant selected on the
earlier period. The later tree-minus-constant Sharpe difference is +0.208,
with a 95% interval of -0.098 to +0.433 using 126-session paired circular
blocks and 2,000 resamples. It includes zero at all three block lengths
(21, 63 and 126 sessions). These intervals do not adjust for the historical
strategy search or multiple comparisons. The evidence is therefore weaker
than a claim that the tree's complexity reliably pays for itself.

Specific design concerns:
- The tree is fitted to full-size, standalone lifetime dollar profit using
  squared error. CEM then chooses leaf sizes to maximize aggregate daily
  shadow-path Sharpe. The final funded portfolio adds shared capital, integer
  contracts, netted hedges, financing and admission constraints. These are
  different objectives and execution environments.
- Training labels retain $0.65 commission per contract, while funded execution
  uses $0.03, a 0.10-vol-point option penalty and 1 bp stock hedge cost.
- Monthly training sets contain 59–299 examples (median 125). Individual
  leaves contain 8–69 examples (median 19); 165 of 582 fitted monthly leaves
  contain fewer than 15. Overlapping trades, pairs and training windows limit
  the amount of independent evidence.
- On shared historical examples, 21.8% of sizing assignments change between
  adjacent monthly fits. This measures model-update sensitivity, not live
  position turnover and not an out-of-sample instability estimate.
- The current tree requests 50% on 316 proposals, 75% on only seven, and 100%
  on 285. Its output is mostly a choice between half and full size.
- There is no zero-size action. The sizing learner cannot reject an otherwise
  qualified opportunity; downstream eligibility and risk checks can.

Assessment: keep the current tree and constant 100% as benchmarks. The two
new simple rules give no reason to replace it. Their failure does not prove
the tree architecture optimal: they also replace CEM, and the linear score
still uses discrete actions. Fully continuous portfolio-aware sizing was not
tested here. Original training and execution code were preserved.

2. ARE THE TRADING OPPORTUNITIES ARBITRARY?

There is a concrete hypothesis: an unusually high or low relative put-IV
spread within a related, liquid equity pair may revert. The code applies a
historical rank rule, a beta/correlation filter, and positive completed-profit
memory. The exact roster, thresholds, contract choices and memory cutoffs
have not been established as optimal or broadly robust.

Three substantive findings matter:

First, the current 345-pair roster is inherited from earlier experiments that
kept pairs with complete simulated paths across their tested variants. The
lineage goes from 358 attempted comparisons to 347 broad pairs, then 346,
then 345. F/GM and DVN/MRO were removed after contract-handling failures
observed in 2023 in earlier models. Other initial availability restrictions
also use full-history coverage. This is retrospective data conditioning.
We found no evidence of direct profit ranking in these removals; the direction
and magnitude of performance bias cannot be determined from this audit.

Second, the near-one beta filter depends on the ordering of the two names.
Reversing the names makes 425 of 608 qualified proposals (69.9%) fail that
same criterion, including 130 of 207 executed entries. That challenges using
the filter as a symmetric measure of pair similarity. It does not establish
that those trades should be removed; requiring both betas near one would
also impose a much stricter correlation threshold.

Third, the profitability memory gate strongly restricts participation:

Cumulative stage                            Pair/date rows    Pairs
Extreme historical spread-rank opportunities      4,744          78
Also meet minimum history counts                  3,484          62
Also have positive profit histories                 608          46
Actually admitted under current sizing              207          44

These counts start inside the already available and filtered candidate data,
not the full 345-pair raw calendar. The memory requirements remove 87.2% of
otherwise eligible extreme-rank opportunities. Of the 608 proposals, 348 are
blocked by risk checks, 50 lack eligible contracts and three fail integer
vega matching. Sizing therefore affects which opportunities get funded.

Existing fixed-100% controls isolate the gate change from learner changes:

Opportunity pool              Earlier Sharpe   Later Sharpe
With profitability memory          0.478           1.055
Without memory requirements        0.847           0.567

Removing the gate helps earlier Sharpe and hurts later Sharpe. Later profit
above cash rises from $200,853 to $330,791, while average reserve rises from
2.49% to 11.84% and cash-relative maximum drawdown grows from 0.65% to 1.24%.
The controls use the same funded settings and calendar; the broader market
cache exactly reproduces the original book on its original contracts. This
does not independently establish the quality of every additional contract.

The direction is relative to the pair's own historical spread. It buys the
higher absolute-IV leg on 22.7% of proposals and 16.4% of entries. That is
consistent with this hypothesis, but the strategy should not be described
universally as buying lower IV and selling higher IV. Independent expiry
selection also produces some periods of unmatched single-leg exposure.

Assessment: the rule has an economic rationale, but universe construction,
pair symmetry and threshold robustness need stronger evidence. Reconstruct
an explicit point-in-time P25 universe and availability rule first. Test a
small number of economically motivated changes with constant sizing before
retraining the sizing policy. The bundled data support the current audit;
they do not support a complete raw-universe reconstruction on their own.

3. DOES THE STRATEGY WORK CONSISTENTLY ACROSS PAIRS?

The later period's $248,673 profit above cash reconciles to $211,262 of pair
trading profit after attributed costs and $37,411 of shared financing versus
the cash benchmark. Shared financing includes interest on earlier strategy
profits and actual option/hedge cash flows; it is not a separate pair alpha.

Later period leaders                 Net pair profit    New entries
SNAP/UBER                                $56,628               4
GAP/TJX                                  $44,041               6
AMD/MU                                   $40,737               9

Those three supply 66.9% of net pair trading profit, or 51.6% of profit from
positive-contributing pairs before offsetting losing pairs. Their positions
fall into only two, two and four overlapping trading episodes respectively.
Several other profitable pairs share industries or underlying stocks.

There are 21 positive and 11 negative pair contributors in the later period.
Across both periods only 12 pairs contribute in each, and three are positive
in both. One of those three has just $14.60 of carried-position profit in the
later period. This does not disprove generalization across pairs; it means
the split offers limited direct evidence of repeated success in the same
pairs. The five largest later winners had no earlier-period funded entries.

Performance is concentrated in time as well:
2022 net pair profit: +$175,530
2023 net pair profit:  +$45,777
2024 net pair profit:  -$30,869
2025 through August:  +$20,824

2022 accounts for 83.1% of later net pair profit and 66 of 110 new entries.
The largest later pair losses are UPST/COIN (-$21,615), UAL/AAL (-$11,765)
and HD/LOW (-$6,921). These are descriptive rankings, not a recommendation
to remove historical losers. A tradable pair-removal result requires a new
portfolio replay because admission, financing and shared hedges would change.

All 207 actual trade cohorts were reconstructed. Daily P&L reconciles within
$0.000000006; pair/year contributions and costs add back to the portfolio.
Netted stock execution and borrow costs use explicit additive allocations;
shared financing remains separate. Open inventory crosses year boundaries.
The saved pair tables include exposure, requested/accepted sizes, episodes,
costs, drawdown contribution and return-variance contribution. Standalone
pair Sharpes are not added to obtain a portfolio Sharpe.

Assessment: the aggregate Sharpe is insufficient evidence of a broadly stable
pair strategy. Pair, industry/underlying, calendar and risk concentration
should accompany every model comparison.

RECOMMENDED NEXT RESEARCH ORDER

1. Establish the opportunity dataset: causal universe membership, explicit
   quote availability, actual put liquidity and a symmetric pair-risk rationale.
2. Use fixed sizing to test whether the proposed signal predicts subsequent
   delta-hedged net profitability, with a small declared set of threshold and
   expiry sensitivities. Preserve all negative results and both memory controls.
3. Align sizing training with the actual funded objective and admission rules.
   Treat abstention and management of existing positions as separately declared
   policy changes. Keep the current tree and constant-size controls throughout.
4. Evaluate performance by pair, period and shared underlying, then use an
   uninspected period for a confirmatory comparison after choices are frozen.

This audit completes a bounded investigation of the three criticisms. It does
not establish globally optimal features, thresholds or sizing, and no strategy
configuration was promoted or changed. Those conclusions would require new
data and explicitly designed follow-up experiments.
