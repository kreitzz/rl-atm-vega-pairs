P25 OPPORTUNITY-SELECTION AUDIT

Scope
Current selected policy: 27 inputs, monthly 18-month training, 50/75/100% sizing,
$10m continuous portfolio, 2018–August 2025. This audit distinguishes the 608
qualified proposals from the 207 trades actually accepted. No tree, selection
rule, model weight, portfolio or checkpoint was changed. Historical results
are exploratory because this history was previously inspected.

Conclusion
The opportunity definition has an economic hypothesis: identify an unusual
relative put-IV spread within a historically related equity pair, trade toward
its usual spread, and require positive completed directional shadow outcomes.
It is concrete and executable in code. That does not validate the exact pair
roster, dates, thresholds, put delta, maturity, or profitability veto. Several
choices are inherited research conventions with material selection limitations.
The most consequential issues are the inherited ex-post complete roster, a
directional beta test that depends on leg ordering, and highly restrictive
profit-history gates whose relative performance changes across time periods.

What determines an opportunity
1. Historical candidate formation: previous 252 sessions; 200 complete input
   observations, median aggregate CALL volume >= 100 and OI >= 500; historical
   three-digit SIC; up to four liquid names per industry; pair return correlation >= 0.4;
   up to 180 stock pairs per two-year screen, rotating industries. Specified ETF
   comparisons are additional candidates. No profit ranking is used here.
   This economically motivates related/liquid pairs, but the coverage measure
   originates in an ATM-call universe rather than the actual P25 puts.
2. The later current 345-pair roster is a subset inherited from older research.
   It is NOT a fresh point-in-time P25 universe. See the lineage below.
3. Inherited observable-pair/formation dates are required. Current code uses
   the DATE INDEX of old rule actions, including zero-action days. It does
   not require the old model to trade or pass a positive-validation gate.
4. Monthly pair filter: preceding 126 return sessions, shifted one session;
   stored first-leg-on-second-leg beta in [0.85, 1.15] and correlation >= 0.5. The
   month-start estimate is frozen for that month. Beta is not market beta.
5. For each leg choose an approximately 30-day 25-delta put. Signal-close selector
   allows 21–45 intended-entry DTE and delta within 0.075 of 0.25. It chooses the
   closest expiry first, then strike/delta distance, width, OI and option ID.
   The two legs' expiries are selected independently. Selection allows OI >= 10;
   final entry requires signal-date OI >= 100 and valid signal/entry quotes.
6. Compute raw IV_A-IV_B percentile in its 252-session rolling history, with
   at least 63 observed quotes. Bottom 20%: long A / short B; above 80%: short A / long B.
   Direction is fixed before the sizing tree. This is relative-to-history
   cheapness, not necessarily buying the lower absolute IV. It is not a fitted
   mean-reversion or cointegration test, nor a causal expected-return estimate.
7. Require positive mean completed shadow payoff for the proposed direction
   over BOTH 91 and 365 calendar days, with at least 21 and 63 observations.
   These are hypothetical daily cohorts, often overlapping, not independent
   traded observations. Completion must be strictly before the signal date.
   Memory retains $0.65/contract training costs; actual funded execution uses
   $0.03/contract. Thus the gate is also an inherited conservative cost hurdle.
8. The tree sizes the qualified proposal. Next-session quote, integer-vega,
   corporate-action and shared risk checks determine whether it is accepted.
   There is no zero-size action in the current selected sizing menu.

Roster lineage and what is not established
The broad preparation code initially requires 504 total IV observations and
20 recorded earnings dates per stock, uses currently cached security-type
metadata, and only covers locally downloaded securities. Even with causal
industry/liquidity formation, these global availability constraints can condition
the historical universe on later coverage. Historical names include acquired
and delisted companies, so this is not simply a current-survivor-only roster.

The original 2018+ actual-contract universe attempted 350 broad pairs plus eight
legacy pairs = 358. The prior expiry/beta comparison retained 347 common-complete
broad pairs. The VIX/momentum comparison retained 346 from that previous roster,
excluding failures across all compared variants. Finally the ATM NN cost-hurdle
comparison retained 345/346, dropping DVN/MRO because its $0.30 NN replay failed
on an unsupported distribution on 2023-02-14. The employee-feature inputs and
subsequent P25 pipeline inherited that 345 roster. Thus the exclusions are broader
than just DVN/MRO and are based on ex-post path completeness in earlier strategies.
This is documented by the original reports and executable roster construction;
there is no evidence here that pairs were directly ranked by realized profit.
The performance bias's direction or magnitude is not established by this audit.
The 347-to-346 exclusion was F/GM: the VIX fixed-size NN could not handle an
adjusted held contract on 2023-02-10. The final DVN/MRO exclusion is therefore
the second directly traced inherited removal, after the earlier broad-roster
completeness restrictions and omission of the eight legacy comparisons.

Formation/context is another historical research choice. uniform_smile_data
uses the index of a prior calendar-control series, and the final transfer
explicitly intersects the old P25 rule-action date index. Old panel availability
and historical membership remain embedded. The original calendar-control
protocol itself calls this retrospective dataset conditioning. Optional current
tree-feature flags are not additional entry vetoes. A full-universe date funnel
cannot be reconstructed solely from the compact bundled training table, because
it already excludes unobserved, beta-ineligible and out-of-context rows.

The memory gate removes 87.2% of otherwise qualified extreme-rank opportunities.
Its profit tests alone remove 82.5% after the count minimums. Of 608 proposals,
279 hit entry risk limits, 69 are blocked while the existing book breaches a
risk limit, 50 lack an eligible contract, and three fail integer vega matching.
The risk_deleveraging label means reject a NEW entry; it does not mean forcibly
sell an existing position. Pair counts differ across stages and overlap across
rejection reasons. Source rows are not the full 345-by-calendar product.

Controlled evidence: memory screen versus no screen
Use the SAME 100% fixed sizing at $10m, so this comparison does not confound the
screen change with the selected 27-input versus 33-input learners or their menus.
                  2018–2021 Sharpe   2022–August 2025 Sharpe
Memory screened          .478                    1.055
No memory screen         .847                     .567

Later-period new entries: 75 screened versus 459 ungated. Profit above cash:
$200,853 screened versus $330,791 ungated. Average reserve: 2.49% versus 11.84%;
maximum cash-relative drawdown magnitude: 0.65% versus 1.24%. Removing the gate
expands exposure and dollar profit but worsens later risk-adjusted performance.
In the earlier period the ungated policy has better Sharpe. These results do
not establish a universally useful screen or optimal 21/63/91/365 cutoffs.

The runners hold the settings, daily stock hedge, $0.03 option commission,
0.1-volpoint fill penalty, 1 bp stock cost, borrowing/dividends, corporate events,
$1m pair/wing reference, account risk limits, and continuous 2018 calendar fixed.
Both preserve open inventory across 2022. The broader pool needs additional
contract quotes; its disk market reads a larger native cache. The archived
baseline check reproduces the original full NAV/trades/decisions. This supports
consistency on original contracts; it does not prove additional contract data
are equally complete or economically interchangeable. Expanded proposals also
compete for the same portfolio risk budget, so this is a portfolio policy
comparison rather than a matched-inventory causal decomposition.

Interpretation of the structural diagnostic
For the same return sample, beta(A|B)=rho*sigmaA/sigmaB and
beta(B|A)=rho^2/beta(A|B). Reversing names can therefore fail the near-one beta
gate even though the underlying pair is unchanged. This challenges using that
gate as a symmetric definition of pair similarity. It does not mean every
reverse-failing pair should be excluded: requiring both directional betas near
one would also impose a substantially higher effective correlation threshold.
A symmetric volatility-ratio feature plus correlation is more transparent to
test, with limits declared before looking at P&L. proposal_structure.csv and
structural_diagnostics.csv measure this issue without choosing winners/losers.
Of the 608 current qualified proposals, 425 (69.9%) fail the near-one beta
criterion when the names are reversed; 130 of 207 accepted entries (62.8%) do.
Median reverse beta is 0.754 across proposals and 0.772 across accepted entries.
The rule also buys the higher absolute-IV leg on 138 proposals (22.7%) and
34 entries (16.4%). This is compatible with the historical-spread rule, but
rules out describing every trade simply as buying low IV and selling high IV.

Maturity is also substantive: saved current-policy diagnostics show 82/608
proposals and 27/207 accepted pairs have different expirations, with up to 21
days of unmatched single-leg exposure. Initial vega matching does not eliminate
gamma, expiry or stock-specific event risk. This is a design assumption, not a
finding that all unmatched trades lose.

Recommendations
1. Rebuild a documented point-in-time P25 candidate universe and replace the
   inherited complete-path roster/date indices with explicit causal availability
   rules. Report unavailable paths as unresolved coverage; do not silently keep
   only full-history successful simulations. Quantify the missing-data effect
   before claiming a robust universe-level result.
2. Define the economic opportunity directly: relative IV/variance-premium
   normalization, symmetric pair risk, actual put liquidity and expiry alignment.
   The current rank rule is a reasonable baseline hypothesis, not demonstrated
   optimal selection. Keep fixed sizing for initial opportunity tests.
3. Retain screened and ungated fixed-size controls in every comparison. The
   existing evidence shows a regime-dependent participation/risk tradeoff; do
   not remove the gate simply because it suppresses many trades or retain it
   simply because the later Sharpe is higher.
4. Predeclare a SMALL, staged sensitivity: symmetric pair-risk definition;
   threshold neighborhood; and expiry alignment, each separately. Refit only
   after isolating opportunity changes; report all variants, pair coverage,
   exposure, costs and P&L alongside Sharpe. A new uninspected time period is
   needed for a confirmatory claim after these exploratory diagnostics.
