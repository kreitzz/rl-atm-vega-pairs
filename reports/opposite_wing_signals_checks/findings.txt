Candidate priorities: C25 absolute wing-IV gap and market-vol-adjusted gap; risk reversal and right-wing steepness as small additions. Oriented absolute IV gap also has a consistent relationship to profitability conditional on the existing spread-rule direction. HAR-RV adds little stable evidence here; realized skew/kurtosis are unstable. Large P75 term correlations have only two qualifying pairs in each broad period and are unsuitable as a general sizing signal.

The strongest P75 fixed-rule result turns negative after excluding its three biggest winning pairs. Both wings remain weak after native profitability memory. Low daily correlations with the .792 put25 policy are useful for investigation but do not establish a profitable combination. P75 and C25 are alternative implementations of the same upside wing, not independent smile sleeves. No new RL was fitted and no strategy combination was selected.

Next experiment: a separate sizing-only learner per wing, with pair-level allocations and shared training where pair histories are short; a bounded ladder around the fixed rule, all observed states scored, optional-feature missing indicators and no model-availability activation veto. Evaluate whether smile/factor features add beyond absolute IV gap. Combine with ATM/put25 only after standalone sizing succeeds, replaying one common inventory book with shared-name risk limits.

Limitations: inspected history, multiple comparisons and overlapping cohorts; chronological subsamples are not a new holdout. Frozen pair/calendar conditioning, initial vega neutrality only, daily delta hedging, no early assignment, no shared-name risk consolidation between current pair sleeves. Canadian dividends use a declaration-time fixed-FX approximation. Quote coverage is substantially thinner for P75. Corporate-action identifier correction is documented in artifacts/contracts/opposite_wing_signals/corporate_accounting.json; frozen .756/.792 references were flat in all affected pairs at that close.
