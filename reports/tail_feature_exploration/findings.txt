Independent short-term tail pair ideas — feature exploration, October 2, 2026

Use separate research silos and eventually separate policies: 30D 25-delta
put/put pairs, 25-delta call/call pairs, 10-delta put/put pairs, and 10-delta
call/call pairs. Keep the frozen ATM reference unchanged. Combining accepted
trading streams is a later portfolio experiment; expanding one policy's action
menu is not this experiment. We have tested many ATM variants, which does not
prove that every possible ATM signal has been exhausted.

This scan has no RL or NN fits and produces no portfolio Sharpe. It explores
18 feature gaps against actual terminal option-pair P&L on the existing pair
roster. All 345 roster members were accounted for; 139 had state panels and
206 had no state panels. Resolved screened opportunities: calls ATM 25,872,
25-delta 10,834, 10-delta 4,465; puts ATM 10,222, 25-delta 10,688,
10-delta 6,125. These are overlapping entry opportunities, not independent
trades. Missing quotes and entry filters substantially reduce tail coverage.

Strongest candidate: 10-delta put pairs and the relative wing-IV spread.
Within-pair, equal-pair-weighted rank correlations with long-A/short-B P&L
relative to the inverse direction:
                            pre-2022 outcomes       2022+ entries
all available                     -0.166                  -0.177
tighter delta matching            -0.151                  -0.127
nonoverlapping schedule           -0.206                  -0.117
controlled rank association       -0.172                  -0.185

The negative direction favors buying A against B when A's wing-IV spread is
relatively lower within the pair's history. Annual rank correlations also
remain negative in each observed year, although the eligible pair count falls
to five in 2024 and two in 2025. On dates shared with ATM puts, the same wing
feature has correlations of -0.162/-0.140 against tail outcomes and
-0.001/-0.083 against ATM outcomes. This suggests a tail-specific lead; it is
not a formal test that tail trading outperforms ATM.

A descriptive entry rule using the bottom/top rolling historical spread
quintiles, long A in the bottom and short A in the top, has mean net terminal
P&L per actual mean initial leg vega of +0.257 before 2022 and +1.641 from
2022. Tight-delta means are +1.320/+1.401. Nonoverlapping means are
-1.434/+4.407: direction prediction is more consistent than profitability.
These are dollar P&L per dollar/vol-point vega, not percentage returns,
annualized returns, trade confidence forecasts, or a portfolio backtest.
The rule was inspected after this feature scan and has no untouched holdout.

Concentration is material. Of pairs with at least eight extreme-quintile
observations, 16/27 have positive mean labels early and 14/23 later. Removing
the three largest positive sums of normalized cohort labels makes the early
mean negative and leaves the later mean only slightly positive. Those sums
describe this opportunity sample, not actual capital-weighted portfolio
profit contributions. The leading later pairs are AMD/MU, SNAP/UBER, and
UPST/COIN. The first prospective test should measure allocation, overlapping
inventory, name limits, and drawdowns rather than infer Sharpe from these labels.

Other leads are weaker. For 10-delta calls, prior 63-day jump fraction and
realized excess kurtosis gaps have raw correlations of -0.064/-0.115 and
-0.063/-0.103, respectively, and survive several controls. Their signs do
not persist across both nonoverlapping samples. For 25-delta calls, wing
steepness has weak negative associations; butterfly historical z-scores lose
most of their association after controls and also relate to ATM outcomes.
For 25-delta puts, downside semivariance and trailing VRP deserve inspection,
but several matched-date or nonoverlapping checks weaken or reverse the raw
relationship. There is no compelling new skew/curvature-only strategy yet.

Feature design: actual OTM puts and calls at same-name, same-expiry anchors;
wing premiums, 25-delta risk reversal and butterfly, 10-to-25 wing steepness,
historical z-scores, 14-to-45 wing term slope, physical return skewness and
excess kurtosis, directional semivariance, jump share, and tail-move magnitude.
Sparse smile curvature is a proxy, not model-free implied skewness/kurtosis.
14/45D observations are features only; each traded silo targets 30D expiry.

Economic labels: same-type/same-target-delta pairs; integer entry sizing,
initial vega mismatch at most 10%, daily delta hedging, each contract held
to its own expiry. Vega is neutral at entry, not rebalanced daily. Costs use
$0.03 option commission, 0.1 vol-point midpoint penalty, stock hedge costs,
and the existing funding/borrow conventions. Labels use the actual ledger
economics, without portfolio reserve/stress/capacity overlays. An ATM exact
recovery control and eight isolated tail-ledger comparisons passed.

All states use signal-close information with next-session execution; ranks
are built before eligibility or future-payoff filtering. Pre-2022 labels must
finish before 2022. Nonoverlap schedules use known expiries, not realized
payoffs. Evaluation remains conditional on resolved histories. Partial rank
regressions are descriptive controls, not trading models. Quarter-block
intervals are exploratory, with no correction for the many feature tests;
shared names and overlapping cohorts remain dependent.

Recommended sequence: test 10-delta put pairs first as a standalone policy
with a small fixed feature set and the existing portfolio risk controls;
then independently test the call-tail jump/kurtosis hypothesis. Compare each
against its simple rule and ATM reference on the same dates. Only then test
whether combining accepted, sufficiently distinct streams improves Sharpe.
