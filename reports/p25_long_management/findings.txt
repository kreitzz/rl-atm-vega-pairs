LONGER SIGNALS, LONGER TRAINING, AND PARTIAL EXITS

The full 30D management strategy improves annualized excess Sharpe from 1.003 to 1.029, with 212 entries and an additional $8,083 above cash over the full period. The original-count control achieves 1.029, retains all 212 cohorts, and has no additional risk rejection. Only 5 cohorts are trimmed and 4 have elective close fills; these groups can overlap. Cash-relative maximum drawdown is slightly worse (0.531% versus 0.491%). This is a modest selected historical improvement, not independent evidence of a large management benefit.

The faster 18M management learner gives 0.963 Sharpe with normal admission, versus 1.040 in its original-count control (211 of 212 cohorts retained; one risk rejection). The latter also has lower excess dollars than expiry holding. Admission and initial-count changes matter, so it cannot be promoted as a live 1.040 strategy from this control alone.

Neither extending entry training to 36M nor slowing the signals improves on the retained entry learner. The 90D hold transfer gives -0.324 Sharpe; management gives 0.046, or 0.025 with its 116 initial cohorts/counts locked. The 30D control on the 213 90D-covered proposal dates gives only 0.262 Sharpe with 74 accepted entries. Coverage selects a substantially weaker sample, and acceptance differs by expiry. This weakens a blanket conclusion that 90D contracts destroy an otherwise identical strong signal. The sensible next long-maturity experiment needs broader 90D quote coverage and 90D-native entry/outcome training.

Missing/crossed quotes and impossible integer trims are explicit rejected instructions.
