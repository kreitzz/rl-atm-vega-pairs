Why the first tail RL test did not implement the spread rule

The RL policy learned freely from two spread inputs; it was not an overlay
that kept the simple rule's entry thresholds/direction and chose only size.
Of its 21 actual full-book entries:
  11 occurred in the middle-rank region where the simple rule is flat.
   7 chose the opposite direction to the raw extreme-spread rule.
   3 agreed with the raw extreme-spread rule.

All seven opposite-direction cases would also have been blocked in the
rule's intended direction by the shared historical profitability gate.
Consequently the actual gated rule would skip 18 of these 21 signal dates;
it would not simply take the opposite side of all seven. These counts compare
signal conditions, not counterfactual realized fills or profits.

18/21 RL entries were at 25% size. All filled entries were in AMD/MU or
BAC/JPM. 18/21 occurred in 2023; the simple rule's major profits were in 2022.
The gate, learned policy, sparse observations and validation selected a very
different opportunity set from the simple rule.

Of 139 pair state panels, 90 ever had valid raw spread plus historical rank;
45 had at least 504 such states across the entire history. A monthly fit
requires at least 504 states in its purged historical training window, so
3,546/4,183 logs report insufficient training. Terminal labels include
next-session quote failures and risk overlays subsequently reject proposals;
the simple feature correlation is not a guarantee of profitable inventory.

A different test could retain the rule's direction/entry conditions and
allow RL only to skip or choose size. That is not what this first fit tested.
Any change to training history requirements or pooling across names would
also be a distinct experiment, not a correction to the saved result.

Actual entry dates, state ranks, directions and memory-gate eligibility are
in rl_entry_rule_alignment.csv. Original fit/actions/ledgers are unchanged.
