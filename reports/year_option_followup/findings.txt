Result: fixed 25% sizing improves annualized cash-excess Sharpe from 0.396012 at fixed 50% to 0.470775. None of the new learned variants beats the fixed 50% reference, much less fixed 25%. This improvement comes from a fixed sizing control; there is no demonstrated one-year RL advantage. Lower size produces less dollar profit.

The same 529 frozen short-dated-policy pair/date/direction proposals are used throughout. Native one-year puts target absolute delta 0.25 and 300–400 days to expiry. There are 230 native training episodes; the old fixed 50% book admits 167 cohorts. Different sizes and management policies change integer contract eligibility and shared risk admissions. These are not proportional leverage transformations of an identical return series. Smaller sizing can fail the unchanged 10% integer-vega matching tolerance; admissions.csv records these exclusions separately from risk-limit exclusions. The fixed 50% book last enters 2024-05-28 and then runs off through 2025-08-29; recent-year results have limited new entries.

Process changes tested: explicit action-output critics; 64 weekly Bellman iterations spanning a year; past-calibrated risk utility; greedy versus uncertainty-penalized inference; native-feature entry Q; quarterly contextual allocation search maximizing shadow-book daily Sharpe; and annual allocation search in the executable shared portfolio. The latter is episodic contextual policy optimization, not sequential fitted-Q/neural RL.

The actual-book learner reaches 0.356002 versus 0.265155 for a matched shadow-trained control using exactly the same state trees, annual cadence, 48-month prefixes and 25/50/100% action menu. Its unadjusted block-bootstrap 95% Sharpe-gain interval contains zero. Actual-book training respects integer fills, shared hedges/cash and original risk admissions, retains true expiry dates, and marks open inventory at the last historical close. A runtime guard rejects future contract price requests. Its improved training objective does not produce an advantage over fixed sizing in evaluation.

A separate fixed-rule holding scan exits the same one-year contracts after 30, 90 or 180 observed trading sessions; fills occur at the next close with observed quotes and normal execution costs. All three underperform.
