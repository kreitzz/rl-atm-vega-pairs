INTERPRETATION
At zero penalty the objective rewards normalized profit without penalizing its squared fluctuations. It does not directly maximize Sharpe. The policy chooses 100% straddles in 138/191 later decisions and its mean straddle target is 73.2%, compared with 46/191 and 24.9% at lambda 0.20, and 31/191 and 16.9% at lambda 1.00. The zero-penalty policy loses $331,255 above cash in 2025; lambda 1 earns $75,335. This supports an explanation involving exposure and allocation timing; it does not establish a causal attribution or predict all future regimes. Fixed 75% straddles achieves later Sharpe 1.084, so excess straddle exposure alone cannot explain the zero-penalty result.

Use lambda zero as the simple failure example in the paper. Call it the profit-only objective, not the validation winner. Increasing the risk penalty trades profit for greater later risk-adjusted performance. Modest positive penalties also have strong validation performance; results are not confined to one isolated setting.
