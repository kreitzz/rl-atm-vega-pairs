\begin{center}\bfseries Abstract\end{center}
\setlength{\parindent}{0pt}\noindent
We study reinforcement learning for two exposure decisions in equity options: sizing individual trades with contextual bandits and allocating capital between strategies with fitted Q-learning. Actual-contract simulations combine approximately 30-day, 25-delta put pairs with short ATM straddles selected by persistent realized-minus-implied volatility. During January 2022--August 2025, learned entry sizing raises annualized excess Sharpe from 1.055 to 1.263 for put pairs and from 0.990 to 1.041 for straddles, relative to full sizing. Combining both learners raises Sharpe from 1.088 to 1.726 in a four-way comparison. With bandit entries, Q also exceeds rolling mean--variance allocation: 1.726 versus 1.519. Interpretation connects entry decisions to completed profitability and volatility, and strategy allocation to the changing reward for straddle risk. Statistical uncertainty and assumed execution quality qualify these historical results.
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:1}
Finding a useful trading signal leaves an investor with a second problem: how much capital should it receive as opportunities and risks change? In options markets, a strategy with favorable risk-adjusted returns can deploy little capital, while a strategy that earns more dollars can expose the account to much larger movements. A ranking of average returns or Sharpe ratios alone does not determine when either strategy deserves funding. We study whether information available at the time of a decision can improve that allocation.

We make the question concrete with two equity-options strategies. The first buys relatively cheap puts and sells relatively expensive puts on related names, using approximately 30-day, 25-delta contracts. It matches vega at entry and adjusts stock delta hedges daily, while retaining differences in realized paths, jumps and company-specific events. The second sells approximately 30-day ATM straddles on names whose completed historical outcomes show persistently low realized volatility relative to entry implied volatility. It also hedges delta daily, but retains net short-gamma and short-vega exposure. These are different sources of option risk: relative pricing across names and premium income against future movement.

Our central learning question is how to allocate capital between the resulting portfolios. A fitted Q-learning policy chooses weekly capital targets using current opportunity measures, completed strategy performance, risk conditions and previous holdings. Entry sizing supplies a supporting decision within each strategy: contextual bandits choose 50\%, 75\% or 100\% of a proposed trade's full size. The economic rules determine proposals, directions and contracts; the entry policies alter commitments and subsequent admission capacity, while the allocation policy changes the capital assigned to each funded strategy index.

We evaluate these decisions through actual-contract simulations with option marks, daily stock hedges, financing and explicit trading charges. A four-way comparison evaluates full-size entries with fixed 50/50 allocation, bandit-sized entries with fixed 50/50 allocation, full-size entries with Q allocation, and bandit-sized entries with Q allocation. All four receive the same signal proposals, and Q is independently refitted for each entry-policy family. During January 2022--August 2025, annualized excess Sharpe rises from 1.088 with full-size entries and fixed 50/50 allocation to 1.726 with both learners. With bandit entries, Q also exceeds rolling mean--variance allocation, 1.726 versus 1.519. Timing and one-period controls investigate whether the allocation result reflects average exposure, conditional information or continuation value; they indicate a larger role for conditional allocation and a smaller incremental sequential benefit. These comparisons describe a profit--risk tradeoff, with no single policy dominating every entry size, risk preference or performance measure.

Both 2018--2021 validation and January 2022--August 2025 evaluation remain visible throughout. Individual fits use strictly completed past outcomes, but strategy development also involved inspection of later-period results. The evaluation therefore describes researched historical policies rather than an independently frozen test of the complete research process. Statistical uncertainty and assumed execution quality qualify the performance gains. The outer allocator also trades fractional claims on separately funded strategy indices; it does not rebuild a shared whole-contract inventory. Our contribution is a controlled examination of adaptive exposure allocation under these assumptions, motivating integrated implementation and prospective evaluation.
\section{Related Work}\label{sec:2}
Ning and Lee \cite{ning2024} develop a reinforcement-learning framework for statistical arbitrage in equity markets. Their approach separates spread construction from trading. They select hedge coefficients by minimizing empirical mean-reversion time, measured as the average duration from a significant local extreme to the next crossing of the sample mean. A tabular Q-learning policy then chooses whether to buy, hold or sell the spread. Its state describes recent price movements by direction and magnitude, and its reward combines the spread's deviation from an equilibrium mean with a transaction-cost term.

The authors train the agent on simulated Ornstein--Uhlenbeck spreads and evaluate the combined spread-construction and trading method on ten US equity pairs, using 2022 for formation and 2023 for trading. They report higher daily Sharpe ratios than the distance-method and Ornstein--Uhlenbeck benchmarks in that sample.

Their analysis focuses on entry and exit within a constructed equity spread; it does not examine capital allocation across distinct options strategies. We study that allocation decision using relative-value put pairs and short ATM straddles, with controlled comparisons that separate learned entry sizing from learned strategy allocation.
