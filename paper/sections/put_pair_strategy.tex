\section{Put Pair Strategy}\label{sec:4}

The put-pair strategy trades relative implied volatility (IV) using approximately 30-day, 25-delta puts on related underlyings. Fixed rules determine trade eligibility, direction and contracts. A regression tree then assigns each proposal to a context, and cross-entropy method (CEM) search determines its entry size independently of the capital-allocation learner.

\subsection{Trading Rules and Position Construction}

Monthly formation restricts the candidate universe to pairs with 126-session return beta between 0.85 and 1.15 and correlation of at least 0.5. For each pair, we rank the IV difference \(D_t=IV_{A,t}-IV_{B,t}\) against the preceding 252 trading sessions, requiring at least 63 observations. At the upper quintile we sell A's put and buy B's; at the lower quintile we reverse direction. These fixed percentile cutoffs measure relative richness against each pair's own spread history.

The proposed direction must also show positive historical profit per initial vega over both 91 and 365 calendar days, requiring at least 21 and 63 completed observations, respectively. These histories use completed simulated standalone candidates, including candidates not admitted to the funded account, and include trading and hedging costs; unfinished outcomes are excluded. The strategy therefore combines an unusual relative IV spread with directional profitability, rather than imposing unconditional spread mean reversion. These entry rules are shared across all put-sizing controls.

Full size targets \$450 of vega per IV point on each leg, using integer option contracts. Vega is measured per one-percentage-point change in IV. Positions match vega at entry and rebalance stock delta daily; vega neutrality is not continuously restored, and gamma, jumps and company-specific risks remain. After entry, the simulator holds the purchased and sold contracts rather than replacing them when daily contract selection changes. Section~\ref{app:contracts} records contract selection and Section~\ref{app:risk} records the admission constraints.

\subsection{Decision-Tree Contexts}\label{sec:11}

After the fixed rules determine a proposal, a regression tree maps its 27 inputs into at most eight leaf contexts. Each monthly fit uses completed examples from the preceding 18 months. Its target is cumulative full-size profit above cash, and splits reduce prediction error. With \(n\) examples, the minimum leaf count is \(\max(8,\min(63,\lfloor n/8\rfloor))\); fewer than 16 examples trigger full-size fallback. The tree defines contexts for sizing; it does not learn the trade-entry thresholds or direction.

The 27-input, 18-month specification was retained from 33 archived configurations ranked on 2018--2021 validation scores. Archived selection used a \$345 million research account; the funded comparisons below use \$10 million. Section~\ref{app:feature-selection} records model selection, and Section~\ref{app:entry-optimization} gives additional estimation settings.

\subsection{CEM Position Sizing}\label{sec:put-cem}

Given the fitted tree, CEM selects a map assigning 50\%, 75\% or 100\% exposure to each leaf context. Both the tree and size map use completed put-pair outcomes, without the Q-allocation reward. Portfolio policies can be learned by optimizing investment objectives directly, including the Sharpe ratio \cite{alphaglass2026}; here the objective is the Sharpe ratio of aggregate completed daily-profit paths.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{entry_policy.pdf}
\caption{The original tree/CEM construction. The three-state maps illustrate candidates; actual trees have up to eight states. Each candidate selects action-specific completed paths; the best 19 update sampling probabilities. The retained map sizes next month's proposals.}\label{fig:entry-policy}
\end{figure}

For completed example \(i\), \(p_{i,d}(a)\) denotes profit above cash on day \(d\) at size \(a\). Each size has its own rounded contracts and simulated hedge path. A map \(m\) assigns size \(a_{\ell(x_i)}\) to the example's tree context. We sum its daily paths, retaining overlapping positions and zero-profit sessions, and score the result:

\begin{equation*}g_d(m)=\sum_i p_{i,d}(a_{\ell(x_i)}),\qquad\widehat S_{\rm train}(m)=\sqrt{252}\,\overline{g}/s_g.\end{equation*}

CEM \cite{deboer2005} updates size probabilities toward the best 19 of 192 candidate maps for 50 rounds. Constant maps and the incumbent are anchors. The retained map sizes the following month's proposals. This standalone-path training score differs from funded-account Sharpe: replay additionally applies shared cash, inventory and admission. Section~\ref{app:entry-optimization} gives exact fitting settings.

The tree/CEM sizing policy improves later excess Sharpe by 0.208 and profit above cash by approximately \$47,800 relative to full size. It admits 110 entries instead of 75.

As specified in Section~\ref{sec:evaluation-protocol}, all policies receive the same 608 put-pair proposals, but size-dependent rounding, inventory and admission produce different funded entries. These comparisons measure complete sizing and admission policies rather than predictive sizing alone.

Most of the put learner's later lead develops in 2022, while 2024 loses ground. Its annualized total account return is 4.667\%; growth relative to cash is 0.530\%. Average reserve is 2.44\% and peak reserve 15.77\%. Cash interest explains much total growth; trading value is measured above cash.

The paired 95\% bootstrap interval for the Sharpe gain of 0.208 is [-0.103, +0.424], conditional on the retained policies and without research-selection adjustment.

SNAP/UBER, GAP/TJX and AMD/MU supply 66.9\% of later net pair trading profit, and 2022 supplies 83.1\%, excluding shared financing. Sections~\ref{app:annual-results} and~\ref{app:additional-put-results} report annual and alternative-sizing results.

\subsubsection{Policy Interpretation and Trade Admission}

\begin{figure}[H]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{put_entry_explanations.pdf}
\caption{Put-pair policy usage during later evaluation: the share of visited tree nodes using each selected input. Frequencies describe fitted policy usage, not causal effects or profit contributions.}\label{fig:put-entry-explanations}\label{fig:entry-explanations}

Put-policy decisions are reconstructed from the saved tree's root-to-leaf conditions and context-to-size map. Exact replay reproduces every saved size across all 608 put proposals. Completed 365-day profitability appears in 18.7\% of later visited decision nodes, mean IV in 14.9\%, commission relative to vega in 12.7\%, and stock-return correlation in 12.3\%. These frequencies describe which information the fitted policy uses; they are not causal effects or a ranking of expected profit contributions.

Later entry overlap with the fixed-full portfolio comprises 74 entries taken by both policies, 36 taken only by learned sizing and one taken only by fixed full size. These differences can arise through the inventory left by earlier size decisions. An explanation of proposed size alone therefore does not explain the entire portfolio result.
