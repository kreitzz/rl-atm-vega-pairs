Part 1 · Formation

EMRT: average time from a big swing back to the mean

Find the meaningful peaks and troughs, time each one back to the mean line, average the times

Xt = S1,t − B·S2,t The spread. Each B gives a different spread; some revert quickly, some drift.
Shaded = stopwatch running (extreme → next mean crossing). A swing counts only if it moves ≥ C·s from the mean (C = 2, s = spread std dev). B is chosen to minimize this EMRT time.
Part 2 · Trading

Trading as an MDP: state, action, reward

The agent trades the fixed spread Xt = S1 − B*S2, one unit at a time

State St
↑+1
↑↑+2
↓−1
↓↓−2
→
St = [+1, +2, −1, −2]
Last l = 4 daily % moves of the spread, each bucketed at k = 3% (small / big, up / down) → 44 = 256 states

Action

  • {sell −1, hold 0, buy +1}
  • Flat → buy or hold; long → sell or hold; never short
Rt+1 = At·(θ − Xt) − c·|At| Paid for trading toward the mean: buy below θ, sell above θ. Uses today's gap, not tomorrow's move.
  • The state carries no level information — only the shape of recent moves.
  • The agent is a price-taker: its trades don't move the spread.
Part 2 · Trading

Q-learning: from one-step rewards to a 256 × 3 table

Each reward only looks at today; the future enters through the next state's Q-value

One step

Rt+1

Reward for today's action

Discounted total

Gt = Σk γk Rt+k+1

= Rt+1 + γGt+1  (random)

Expected total

Q(s,a) = E[Gt | s, a]

One fixed number per cell

Q(St,At) ← Q(St,At) + α·[ Rt+1 + γ·maxa Q(St+1,a) − Q(St,At) ] The bracketed target R + γ·max Q(St+1) is a one-step stand-in for Gt (bootstrapping). Q converges; it doesn't accumulate.
α = 0.1
learning rate
γ = 0.99
discount factor
ε = 0.1 → 0

Part 2 · Aside

Why the agent trains on simulated spreads

The reward needs something you never observe in real markets

Problem

  • The reward uses the true mean θ — unknown on real data
  • One real price path is far too little data to fill 256 × 3 cells

Fix

  • Simulate 10,000 one-year OU paths with known θ (μ=1, θ=1, σ=0.1)
  • Train the Q-table there, then trade the real 2023 spread with it
  • All headline results that follow are on real stock data; only training is synthetic.

Our Extension

State: let a regression tree pick the buckets

Fit a small tree to predict the reward from features — its leaves become the discrete states

Illustrative split (4 leaves shown). Real trees use 4, 8, or 16 leaves — whichever scores best on validation Sharpe — with a minimum of 126 or 252 dates per leaf.
  • Inputs: IV spread, both legs' earnings countdown, both legs' trailing 30-day RV, and VIX.
  • The tree is fit once, on training data only, to predict the reward — it's never used to trade directly, only to draw the state boundaries.
  • Contrast with the paper: their buckets are four fixed thresholds on daily return, picked by hand; ours are chosen by what actually predicts the reward.

Our Extension

Action and reward, adapted for options

Same spirit as the paper's reward — position × distance from favorable value, minus a cost term

payoff = size × [(RVfwd,A−IVA) − (RVfwd,B−IVB)] − |size| × 0.025 × (IVA+IVB)/2 size ∈ {0, ±0.25, ±0.50, ±0.75, ±1.00} × per-pair vega budget — a 9-level signed sizing ladder
  • Paper's binary buy/sell becomes a 9-level sizing ladder (0/25/50/75/100% × direction), no position gating.
  • Cost mirrors the paper's flat c·|At| but is vega-scaled: 2.5% of average entry IV, proportional to size.
  • The reward — forward 30-day RV minus IV — is something we actually observe 30 days later. Unlike the paper's θ, there's no unknown to estimate, so there's nothing to simulate: we train directly on real history.

Our Extension

Training: a contextual bandit, not Q-learning

Every date is one independent decision — no persisted position, no next-state value to bootstrap

Sample

256 policies

one of 9 actions per state, drawn from that state's current action distribution

Score

Sharpe(a)

the whole candidate policy, scored at once — see formula below

Reshape

Keep the elites

nudge each state's action distribution toward the top ~10%'s choices

mean(a) = (1/n) Σs [ as·Rs − |as|·Cs ]     Sharpe(a) = ANN × mean(a) / std(a) a = (a1,…,aS) is one candidate policy, one of the 9 sizes per state. Rs, Cs = total reward and total cost already summed over every training date in state s, computed once — so scoring any policy is just arithmetic over S numbers, not a scan over the data.
  • Why this needs a search, not a per-state formula: mean(a) is a plain sum across states, but Sharpe divides by the policy's standard deviation, which depends on mean(a)2 — a square of a sum over every state. So the 9-way choice in one state changes the score of every other state's choice too; there's no independent "best action for this state" to just read off.
  • State → action → reward realized 30 days later, no transitions or bootstrapped next-state estimate — the population search (80 iterations × 5 seeds) is what stands in for Q-learning's update rule here.

Our Extension

One search iteration, visually

256 candidate policies sampled, scored by Sharpe, then the top ~10% reshape each state's distribution

Illustrative: 5 of the 16 states shown, one row per state, 9 columns = the 9 sizes. Shading = how likely that action is to be sampled for that state right now.
  • Each of the 256 candidates is a full length-16 vector — one action drawn per state — not a single state-action pair.
  • The elites don't have to agree with each other on every state; each state's distribution only shifts toward whatever that state's winners picked.
  • Repeat 80 times: distributions start flat, and sharpen round by round until each state settles on essentially one dominant action.
Our Extension

Train / validation / test — and why we retrain

The paper fits once on a fixed formation year; we re-fit on an expanding window as new data arrives

train — fit tree + CEM policy
validation — pick leaf-count / min-leaf
test — scored once, out-of-sample
Illustrative: 3 of the folds shown. Test windows are fixed-length and never overlap; the training window keeps expanding, sliding forward through Aug 2025.
  • Paper: one formation year (2022) — fits θ, the reversion speed, and hedge ratio B from real prices, then trains the Q-table on 10,000 simulated OU paths. The table is frozen for all of 2023; nothing is re-estimated mid-year. Chronologically clean (no future data touches the fit), but if the true dynamics drift during 2023, the frozen table has no way to notice.
  • Ours: every fold re-fits the tree and re-runs the cross-entropy search from scratch on an expanding training window, picks the leaf-count / min-leaf combo by validation Sharpe, then scores that one frozen policy once on the next untouched test slice before sliding forward.

Our Extension

The baseline: a small neural network

Same 7 features as the tree, but one fixed-size in/out signal instead of 9 graded sizes

Fixed unit size or nothing — never the 9-level ladder the RL policy gets to use.
L(w) = (1/n) Σi (ŷi − yi)²    +    α·‖w‖² yi = standardized long_reward label, ŷi = network output. Minimized directly by lbfgs — Sharpe never enters training; it only picks α ∈ {0.01, 1} and the threshold k afterward, on validation.
  • 7 standardized features → 1 hidden layer, 16 tanh units → 1 output — regressing the forward reward directly, not classifying among 9 sizes.
  • A separate rule, not the network, turns that number into a trade: above a cost-scaled threshold, take a fixed ±1 position; below it, stay flat.
  • Same model-selection discipline as the tree: 2 weight-decay strengths × 3 thresholds = 6 candidates, best one chosen by validation Sharpe, applied once to test.

Honest Limitations

Caveats on both sides

Neither result should be over-read

Paper

  • Single formation/trading split (2022 → 2023) — one market regime, no walk-forward
  • 10 hand-picked sector pairs, no significance testing across pairs
  • Reward needs the true θ, only available via simulation transfer — a sim-to-real gap

Ours

  • Sharpe is a 30-day proxy payoff, not daily marked option-and-hedge P&L (the contract pilot addresses this separately for AMD/NVDA only)
  • Only AMD/NVDA shows a robust standalone edge; the other three pairs are individually negative
  • Training-window sensitivity is material: adding features shrank the training window and changed AMD/NVDA's own Sharpe from 0.635 to 0.369 in a like-for-like refit
  • Broader universe, ex-silver, loses in 4 of the last 4 years despite a positive full-period score

Sequential Q Extension · 1/2

What the agent sees, and what it can do

The bandit re-decides fresh every day; this agent manages an open trade over time, so it has to remember more

State

  • Same tree as the bandit sorts today's market — spread, vol, earnings — into a handful of buckets.
  • Plus, if a trade is already open: which direction, how many days it's been held, and whether it's currently working out or not.
  • Being flat is its own simple situation, with none of that extra baggage.

Action

  • Flat: stay out, or enter long or short.
  • Holding: keep holding, or exit.
  • Never both at once — you can't add to a position you're already in.
  • Every (state, action) pair gets one entry in the Q-table — the model's job is to fill in which action looks best from each state.
Sequential Q Extension · 2/2

How the table gets filled in

Not learned by trial and error — solved directly, because the whole history is already on hand

Today's outcome

The reward

Entering or exiting costs a small fee; holding earns or loses that day's P&L; staying flat costs nothing.

Averaged across history

One stable estimate

Every past day that shared this exact state and action is pooled together, so one noisy day becomes a dependable average.

Solved, not learned

The whole table at once

Repeatedly updated from itself until nothing moves — not nudged forward one lived experience at a time.

R̂(s,a) = 1N Σi ri      Q(s,a) = R̂(s,a) + γ·maxa′ Q(s′,a′) R̂(s,a) pools the one-day reward across every historical instance of this exact state and action into a single average. Q(s,a) then adds the discounted value of whatever the best action leads to next — solved by sweeping this equation across the whole table until it stops changing, no learning rate involved.
  • Still walk-forward safe: training only ever sees data from before validation and test. Reusing the same historical days under different hypothetical trades adds coverage — it doesn't add new independent market days.
Future Work

Where the paper's other ideas could still plug in

We've borrowed the trading-phase philosophy; the formation-phase machinery is still on the table

  • EMRT-style pair/weight search for vol spreads: instead of a fixed 1:1 vega-neutral pairing, grid-search relative option notional to minimize the IV spread's empirical reversion time.
  • Sequential Q extension: the preceding two slides show our implemented batch Q learner. Next: jointly forecast both legs’ realized variance and test a risk-adjusted reward. These remain proposals, separate from the main bandit results.
  • Daily marked P&L for all four pairs, extending the AMD/NVDA contract pilot, to replace the proxy-payoff Sharpe with realized, cost-inclusive returns.
  • Cross-pair capital allocation: today each pair is independent with a fixed budget; a joint policy across pairs is the natural next step (already partially explored, see independent_diagnostics.md).

What the learner does

Learn size within a focused strategy

Use precise algorithm labels: contextual allocation and sequential management are distinct

  • 30-day sizing: tree states plus direct policy search; actions are 50%, 75% or 100%.
  • Six-month management: fitted Q learns weekly hold, half-size or close decisions.
  • New one-year sizing: quarterly completed-history fits choose 0%, 25%, 50% or 100%.
  • Execution remains subject to quotes, integer contracts and common account risk checks.
The successful short-dated method is a contextual tree plus CEM optimized for portfolio Sharpe, not a neural sequential Q learner. The new native one-year method is a small-state contextual episodic direct policy optimization. The six-month fitted-Q experiment uses Bellman updates and is sequential. Claim adaptive policy value where the matched benchmark improves, rather than treating every method as the same RL algorithm.

Interpretation

Adaptive decisions help within specific strategies

Separate economic signal, learned sizing, sequential management, and portfolio allocation

  • Strongest evidence: P25 short-dated sizing improves common fixed-vega controls.
  • Smaller gains: six-month management and native one-year sizing; uncertainty remains broad.
  • Limits: weak opposite-wing results, failed direction learning, and weaker historical portfolio optimization.
  • Next test: freeze the design and evaluate new dates with prospective pair formation.
The report should not claim that RL always wins, that all smile regions have the same signal, or that the contract strategy has a newly matched neural-network comparison.
